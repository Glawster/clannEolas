## Before acting

The time bands below prioritise work; they are not statutory deadlines. The
institution, estate, urgency and applicable jurisdiction determine what is
needed. Do not use the deceased person's card, PIN, app or online-banking login,
even when those credentials are known.

`Tell Us Once` notifies specified government bodies and some public-sector
pension schemes. It does not generally notify private banks, card issuers,
insurers, utilities or private pension schemes. These require separate review.

## Immediate safeguarding, usually within 48 hours

- Preserve statements, post, devices, cards and records without attempting
  access through the deceased person's credentials.
- Identify immediate funeral, dependant, housing, care and fraud risks.
- Use the continuity graph to find income and essential commitments funded by
  accounts that may be restricted.
- Do not cancel every payment or close accounts before contracts, joint holders,
  income and essential services are understood.
- Contact an independently verified fraud route if unauthorised use is suspected.

## Initial administration, usually within the first week

- Identify the executor, administrator or jurisdiction-equivalent route and the
  evidence institutions request.
- Notify known institutions through their bereavement process and record the
  contact, reference, documents requested and confirmed account treatment.
- Review sole and joint accounts, payment instructions, cards, overdrafts,
  safe-deposit arrangements and essential incoming/outgoing payments.
- Ask each provider how essential bills, funeral invoices and urgent estate
  costs can lawfully be handled; do not assume funds can be withdrawn.
- Notify private income providers not covered by government services where an
  overpayment, suspension or redirection may arise.

## Information gathering, usually within the first month

- Request date-of-death balances, accrued interest, liabilities and transaction
  evidence needed for estate and tax work.
- Review each income and outgoing commitment for continuation, overpayment,
  transfer, settlement or authorised cancellation.
- Search statements, tax records and post for unknown relationships. Use an
  approved tracing route such as My Lost Account for relevant UK bank/building-
  society searches.
- Record whether the provider requires probate, confirmation or another grant;
  thresholds and processes vary by provider and jurisdiction.
- Preserve joint-account contribution and beneficial-ownership evidence rather
  than treating survivor access as conclusive.

## Estate administration

- Maintain an inventory of accounts, date-of-death values, post-death income,
  liabilities, notifications, proceeds, closures and transfers.
- Keep estate money and records separate from personal money and preserve the
  authority and evidence for every transaction.
- Reconcile provider proceeds and investigate differences before distribution.
- Retain statements, tax documents, payment and closure evidence for the
  applicable retention period.
- Review banking-licence aggregation and temporary high balances against the
  current [deposit-protection guidance](depositProtection.md).
- Close or transfer an account only after its income, payments, cards, debts,
  products, tax and evidence dependencies are resolved.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Treating next of kin as transaction authority.
- Using the deceased person's payment instrument or credentials.
- Paying or distributing estate funds before liability and authority are clear.
- Cancelling payment mandates without addressing the underlying contracts.
- Assuming joint operational access settles beneficial ownership or tax.
- Assuming Tell Us Once notified private organisations.
- Trusting contact or payment details from an unexpected message.
- Closing accounts before date-of-death, tax and reconciliation evidence is
  secured.
