## Core cautions

A joint account combines several questions that must remain separate:

- who is named as a legal account holder;
- who contributed funds and has beneficial ownership;
- whether either holder or all holders must authorise transactions;
- what the provider permits after incapacity, dispute or death;
- what belongs in an estate and how it is treated for tax; and
- which income and essential commitments depend on the account.

Operational access is not conclusive evidence of beneficial ownership, estate
treatment or tax liability.

## Incapacity

If a holder may lack capacity, contact the provider and verify the applicable
authority and mandate. A provider may restrict some or all operations while it
establishes who may act. A joint holder must not assume their ordinary access
authorises decisions for the other holder. Record essential payments and income
that could be affected and the provider's confirmed decision.

## Death

UK consumer guidance says a joint account will normally continue in the names of
surviving holders, but the provider, mandate, ownership evidence and applicable
law control the actual outcome. Notify the provider, obtain a date-of-death
balance and preserve contribution/ownership evidence. Estate and tax review may
remain necessary even when a survivor can continue using the account.

## Eolas guidance

- Record legal holders, beneficial interests, contribution evidence and signing
  rule independently.
- Never infer survivorship or ownership merely from continued account access.
- Traverse incoming and outgoing continuity dependencies before a restriction,
  transfer or closure.
- Record provider decisions with date, source and event; do not turn one
  provider's process into a universal rule.
- Escalate disputes, unusual contribution patterns, trust arrangements and
  cross-border accounts for professional review.
