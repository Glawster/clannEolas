# Project principles

Principles are clanneolas.com's north star. The product vision explains why the
project exists, requirements define what outcomes are needed, and ADRs preserve
why important choices were made. Principles guide all three.

When options compete, contributors should use these principles to choose the
approach that best serves families. A proposal that conflicts with a principle
must identify the conflict explicitly and normally requires an ADR.

## P-001: People before paperwork

The handbook exists to reduce the burden on people facing illness, incapacity,
emergency or bereavement. Administrative completeness is never more important
than clarity, dignity and the reader's immediate needs.

We prioritise the information and actions that genuinely help. We avoid long
forms, unnecessary detail and language that makes people feel they have failed
when a section is incomplete or not applicable.

## P-002: Help families continue confidently

The handbook should help trusted people understand what matters, what needs
attention and where to find reliable information. It should reduce uncertainty
without pretending to replace judgement or qualified professional advice.

Success means a family can take the next appropriate step with greater
confidence—not that every possible fact has been collected.

## P-003: Explain before asking

Before asking for information, explain why it may help, how it may be used, how
sensitive it is and whether a safer reference would be enough. People should be
able to make an informed choice about what they record.

Prompts use plain language, make optionality clear and distinguish facts,
wishes, actions and professional advice.

## P-004: Privacy by default

Collect the minimum information needed for a clear purpose. Default to the safer
choice when disclosure, storage or sharing could cause harm. Public templates
and private household information remain separate.

Privacy is part of content design, not a warning added after the handbook is
complete.

## P-005: Technology should disappear

Technology may remove effort, but it must not become another problem a family
has to solve. Core content uses open, human-readable formats, works offline
where practical and avoids vendor lock-in.

If software is built later, it should support the handbook quietly. The family
should not need to understand the software architecture, maintain a service or
recover an account before they can use essential information.

## P-006: The handbook must remain useful on paper

A printed handbook is a first-class form of the product, not a degraded export.
Core information must be legible, navigable and understandable without links,
scripts, interactive controls or an internet connection.

Classification labels and essential context remain visible when printed.

## P-007: Reference sensitive information; never reproduce secrets

Sensitive information should normally be referenced by a safe, proportionate
location rather than copied. Passwords, PINs, recovery codes and equivalent
secrets never belong in ordinary handbook content.

The handbook can explain that a separate authorised access arrangement exists
without becoming the credential store itself.

## P-008: Public project, private household

The public repository contains reusable guidance, blank templates and clearly
fictional examples. Real household data belongs in a separate private location.

Contributors must be able to improve the shared project without exposing the
people it is intended to help.

## P-009: Open and adaptable

The project uses open formats and avoids assumptions that bind it to one vendor,
application, household type or jurisdiction. Initial guidance may be
UK-oriented, but country-specific rules must remain separable from the core
household model.

## P-010: Accessible by design

Language, structure and layouts should work for people with different reading
levels, abilities, devices and circumstances. Accessibility is considered when
content is proposed, not tested only after completion.

Stress and grief can make any interface harder to understand. Clear headings,
short explanations and predictable structure help everyone.

## Applying the principles

Each requirement links to the principles it advances. Reviews should test the
actual outcome against those principles rather than treating the IDs as tags.
When principles create a genuine tension—for example, useful detail versus data
minimisation—the requirement or ADR should explain the trade-off.
